Interstellar Comet 3I/ATLAS, also known as comet C/2025 N1 (ATLAS), is a rare interstellar visitor that originated from outside our solar system. Discovered in July 2025, this celestial body offers unprecedented insights into the composition and formation of objects in distant star systems. Currently, it resides approximately 670 million kilometers (420 million miles) from the Sun.

As it continues its journey through our solar system, Comet 3I/ATLAS will make its closest approach to the Sun in late October, passing closer to Mars than Earth. This comet is only the third interstellar object ever observed, following the interstellar asteroid 1I/ʻOumuamua in 2017 and the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov in 2019.

While Comet 3I/ATLAS is not expected to collide with Earth, it presents a unique opportunity for astronomers to study the properties of interstellar objects up close. The comet is currently being tracked by several observatories around the world, and updates on its status, position, and images are regularly shared online.

The arrival of Comet 3I/ATLAS serves as a reminder of the vastness of the universe and the ongoing search for answers about our place in it. As we continue to observe and learn from interstellar visitors like Comet 3I/ATLAS, we gain valuable insights into the origins of our solar system and the processes that govern celestial bodies beyond it.
