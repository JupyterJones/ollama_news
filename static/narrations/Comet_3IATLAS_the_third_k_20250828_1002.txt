Comet 3I/ATLAS, the third known object from outside our solar system to be discovered, was first reported by the NASA-funded ATLAS survey telescope in Chile on July 1, 2025. Astronomers have categorized this object as interstellar due to its hyperbolic orbital path, which indicates it originated from outside our solar system and will not be bound by the Sun's gravity.

Since its discovery, observations made before July 1 were collected from archives of four different telescopes worldwide, extending back to June 14. The comet poses no threat to Earth and will remain far away, with its closest approach being approximately 1.8 astronomical units (about 170 million miles or 270 million kilometers) in October 2025.

NASA's Hubble Space Telescope has provided valuable insights into the size of 3I/ATLAS, estimating an upper limit on its diameter of 3.5 miles (5.6 kilometers), although it could be as small as 1,000 feet (320 meters) across. The comet's active nature, with an icy nucleus and coma, classifies it as a comet rather than an asteroid.

As 3I/ATLAS approaches the Sun in October, its speed will increase, making it an exciting subject of study for astronomers around the world. The comet should remain visible to telescopes through September 2025 before passing too close to the Sun to observe. It will reappear on the other side of the Sun by early December, allowing for renewed observations.

With the discovery and continued study of interstellar objects like 3I/ATLAS, humanity continues to unravel the mysteries of the universe and expand our understanding of the cosmos beyond our own solar system.
