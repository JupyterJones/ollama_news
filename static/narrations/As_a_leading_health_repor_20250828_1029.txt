As a leading health reporter, I've been closely monitoring the situation with President Trump and his recurring hand bruising. Since becoming president at an age when such concerns are heightened—seven decades old in January last year—he has shown no cognitive decline according to his physician but remains fixated on playing golf frequently, which could be exacerbating the issue.

The initial White House insistence that handshaking and aspirin use were behind these bruises was not sufficient evidence for a chronic condition like venous insufficiency or arterial disease as suggested by his physician's later revelation of this diagnosis, which is quite common in the over 70 age group.

Trump has faced similar situations before—like blister-marked hands last January and persistent red markings on other occasions—that have invited speculation about a range of causes from syphilis to allergies or hand grip issues, but no confirmed health concerns followed these events besides possible dry skin. Now again in the White House with visible bruising after an executive order signing has reignited debates regarding his physical condition and cognitive abilities amidst his recent campaign speeches that some perceive as rambling rather than a conscious speaking style he often boasts about, possibly hinting at underlying health issues affecting memory.

Expert opinions have been mixed; while Trump's physician assures him is in "excellent cognitive and physical health," other medical professionals might want to evaluate the bruising alongside his age-related circulatory concerns more closely for a comprehensive understanding of President Trump’s overall wellbeing.
